# 0025. Pack: what it removes, batches, and the progress record

Status: accepted. One point waits for the human's confirmation: F8 said "without blocking reads or writes", and a commit now waits for at most one batch of a pack (see Consequences).

## Context

F8 asks for pack with garbage collection, online, without blocking reads or writes. F11 asks that nothing is undoable past the last pack, F26 for a dry-run report, and N1 that a crash loses nothing that was acknowledged.

redb has one writer, so every removal is a write transaction that a commit waits for. `history` and the iterator read the transaction rows and the records in separate snapshots, so every snapshot must keep them consistent.

## Decision

What pack keeps, the roots of its mark, the rows that go, the watermark and the blob order: specification, "Storage core", "Pack".

**The pack tid.** A pack at `at` packs at T, the lower of `at` and the last tid. A commit during the pack gets a tid after T, so only the pack changes the records at or before T.

**References.** `zoe_pickle::persistent_ids` reads the persistent id of each BINPERSID and PERSID of both pickles, opaque parts included, as ZODB's `referencesf` does. It decodes the record with a node budget equal to the record length, and never guesses. A reachable record that does not decode fails the pack with `Format`, with its oid and tid, before any removal. Such a record that a commit writes during the pack stops the pack before its next batch. The error is `Format`, not a new variant, because the in-process storage matches every variant of `ZoeError`.

**Transaction rows.** The write that removes records lowers the oid count of their rows and removes their index keys (ADR 0026). So in every snapshot the oid count of a row equals its records and its index keys. `history` leaves out a record that a pack removed between two reads.

**Batches.** A key scan selects the records in key order, the records of one oid from the oldest. A batch holds at most 1,000 records for `Store::pack` and is one backend write under the commit sequencer. While a pack runs, each finish notes its tid and its oids after its backend commit. Before each batch, the pack marks what the noted records refer to, once outside the sequencer and again under it, so no finish comes between that mark and the write.

**Errors.** A failed batch write halts the store. A record that fails its checksum stops the pack with `CorruptRecord`, also when nothing reaches it. A read-only store refuses a pack with `ReadOnly`, a halted store with `Halted`.

**The progress record.** The watermark, the intent files of ADR 0023 and the batches are the whole progress record. After a batch write, the pack removes the blob files, syncs their directories, then removes the intent files. Each batch is atomic, so a crash between two batches leaves a store that opens without repair, and a later pack at the same tid removes the rest.

**A stop.** `Store::pack_until(at, stop)` calls `stop` after each batch, and ends with `Closed` when it returns true. `Closed` is not a new variant, for the reason of `Format`. The mark does not check the stop, so at most one batch runs after it. `zoe serve` stops a pack this way, and nothing else does. The watermark means that a pack may have removed records at or before it: the iterator skips a transaction that is gone at or before the watermark, and reads one that is still there.

**The dry run.** `Store::pack_dry_run(at)` runs the mark, the plan and the confirmation of the pack, the same functions, and reports each record where the pack removes it. With no commit between them, the dry run and the pack give equal reports. It writes nothing, holds the commit sequencer only to read the last tid, refuses a halted store, runs on a read-only store, and takes the lock of a pack. The report gives the records, the objects and the blob files with their bytes, and the count and the bytes of each class. A class is `module.name` from the class pickle alone. A deletion, a persistent class and a class pickle that does not decode go under `<unknown>`, which no class name equals.

## Consequences

- Deletion is immediate, until the retention window of phase 7.
- F8 changes, to be confirmed by the human: reads never wait, and a commit waits for at most one batch of a pack. The batch size bounds the wait.
- The mark holds the reachable oids in memory, about 10 to 20 bytes each. The decode of one record can take about 150 bytes per byte of record.
- A stop of `zoe serve` during the mark waits for the mark and the first batch, which can last past `--shutdown-timeout`.
- A reachable record that does not decode, or a record that fails its checksum, blocks every pack until it is repaired.
- Every pack reads all the record keys. It decodes every record after T and the newest record at or before T of each reachable oid.
- `Backend::pack(before, reachable)` is not used. Its removal changes an existing trait, so it is a decision of the human.

## Alternatives

- **Deletion markers.** A row for each removed object forever, listed by `history` and the iterator, that restores nothing.
- **Rows kept with a stale count.** The iterator could no longer detect a lost record.
- **The whole pack in one write.** It stops every commit for the whole pack and loses all the work at a crash.
- **Reachability at T only, as FileStorage does.** A reader after T could miss an object that a later record links again.
