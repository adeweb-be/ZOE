# 0025. Pack: what it removes, batches, and the progress record

Status: accepted. One point waits for the human's confirmation: F8 said "without blocking reads or writes", and a commit now waits for at most one batch of a pack (see Consequences).

## Context

F8 asks for pack with garbage collection, online, without blocking reads or writes. F11 asks that nothing is undoable past the last pack. F26 asks for pack safety: the dry-run report in phase 1, the retention window and incremental pack in phase 6. N1 asks that a crash at any instruction loses nothing that was acknowledged. Pack is a red zone (ADR 0022), because it deletes data.

The specification planned three things that the build changes. Pack walked `records` and `txns` in reverse. A 0-length payload marked a deletion by pack. Writers paused only for the final meta update.

redb has one writer. Every removal is a write transaction, and a commit waits while it runs. `history` and the transaction iterator read the transaction rows and the records in separate snapshots, so pack must keep the rows and the records consistent in every snapshot.

ZODB's FileStorage keeps only what is reachable from the root at the pack time, and the back pointers of later undo records. A record after the pack time that links an object again does not keep it.

## Decision

**The pack tid.** A pack at `at` packs at T, the lower of `at` and the last tid. Every commit during the pack gets a tid after T, so the records at or before T change only by the removals of the pack.

**What pack keeps.** Pack keeps every record after T. For each oid that a reader at or after T can reach, it keeps the newest record at or before T, a deletion record included. It removes every other record at or before T, then its blob record, then its blob file. It removes rows and writes no deletion record.

**The roots.** The roots of the mark are the root oid and every oid that a record in (T, last tid at the start of the pack] refers to. From each root, the mark follows the references of the newest record at or before T. Weak references (`w`) and references into another database (`m`, `n`) keep nothing.

**References.** References come from `zoe_pickle::persistent_ids`, which is equivalent to ZODB's `referencesf`: it reads the persistent id of every BINPERSID and PERSID of both pickles, the opaque parts included, and its tests compare it with `referencesf` on the corpus and on hand-built records. It decodes the record first, with a node budget equal to the record length. Any refusal is a hard error and never a guess. A reachable record that does not decode fails the pack with `Format`, whose text names the oid and the tid of the record, before the pack removes anything. The error is `Format` and not a new variant, because the in-process storage matches every variant of `ZoeError`. Such a record that a commit writes during the pack stops the pack before its next batch.

**Transaction rows.** In the same backend write that removes records, the oid count of each transaction row drops to the records that remain, and a row with none left is removed. So in every snapshot the oid count of a row equals its records. The iterator reads each transaction with its records in one snapshot, and `history` leaves out a record that a pack removed with its row between two reads.

**The watermark.** The first batch writes `pack_watermark` in `meta`, the key that the data model names, in the same write as its removals. A pack at a tid below the watermark does nothing. A store that was never packed has no such row and reads as 0.

**Batches.** A key scan selects the records to remove in key order, the records of one oid from the oldest, so the newest record at or before T of an oid goes last. A batch holds at most a given number of records, 1,000 for `Store::pack`. Each batch is one backend write, under the commit sequencer. While a pack runs, each finish notes its tid and its oids under the sequencer, after its backend commit. Before each batch, the pack marks what the noted records refer to: once outside the sequencer, then again under it, so no finish comes between that mark and the write. A record durable before the batch that would remove the object that it links keeps that object. A finish waits for at most one batch. Readers never wait.

**Errors.** An error of the backend write of a batch leaves its outcome unknown, so the store halts, as after a failed finish. A record that fails its checksum stops the pack with `CorruptRecord` and its oid and tid, also when nothing can reach it: a pack must not delete the evidence of a corruption. A read-only store refuses a pack with `ReadOnly`, and a halted store with `Halted`.

**Blob order.** Before the backend write of a batch, the pack writes and syncs an intent file in `blobs/tmp/` for each tid of the blob records that it removes, in the format of ADR 0023. After the write, it removes the blob files, syncs their directories, also of a file that is already gone, and then removes the intent files.

**The progress record.** The watermark, the intent files and the batches are the whole progress record. Each batch is atomic and correct alone, so a crash between two batches leaves a store that opens without repair. The next open removes the blob files that an intent file names and that have no blob record. A later pack at the same tid removes what the crashed pack left. No other key is needed.

## Consequences

- Deletion is immediate. Nothing undoes a pack until the retention window of phase 6.
- A client that links an object from a reference that it read before T can link an object that an earlier batch removed. The reference then dangles, as with FileStorage. A client whose snapshot is at or after T cannot do this.
- F8 changes, to be confirmed by the human: reads never wait, and a commit waits for at most one batch of a pack, not only for a final meta update. The batch size bounds the wait.
- Memory grows with the reachable set: the mark holds the reachable oids in memory, in the order of 10 to 20 bytes each. The pack decodes one record at a time, and with a budget equal to the record length its tree can take up to about 150 bytes per byte of record. The class names of the report come from the class pickle alone.
- A reachable record that does not decode blocks every pack until the record is repaired. So does a record that fails its checksum.
- Every pack reads all the record keys, because no key space maps a tid to its oids. It decodes every record after T and the newest record at or before T of each reachable oid.
- `Backend::pack(before, reachable)` is not used. It stays only because a removal changes an existing trait, which the standing rule leaves to the human. Its removal is the recommended next step.

## Alternatives

- **Deletion markers.** A 0-length record for each removed object, as the specification planned. The store would keep a row for each removed object forever, `history` and the iterator would list the markers, and a marker would not let anything restore the removed state. FileStorage also removes the records.
- **Rows kept with a stale count.** The rows of removed records would stay unchanged. The iterator would then find fewer records than the oid count of a row, and its check would have to go or to accept a lower count, so it could no longer detect a lost record.
- **The whole pack in one write.** One backend write for the whole pack would stop every commit for the whole pack, hold the whole removal in one write transaction, and lose all the work at a crash.
- **Reachability at T only, as FileStorage does.** A reader after T could then miss an object that a record after T links again.
