# ZOE-173 handoff

## State

- Branch `card/ZOE-173` holds the acceptance tests of the card and one change of `crates/zoe-core/src/redb_backend.rs`. The format is unchanged. The number of syncs is unchanged.
- `crates/zoe-core/tests/finish_time.rs` measures `finish` at concurrency 1 with 100 records of 1 KB per transaction, in four scenarios: `add` and `update`, each with compression off and with zstd. It prints the time of `finish`, of the span `fsync` (N17), of the syncs and of the writes of the database file, the bytes written, and `finish` less its syncs. It is ignored: run it in release (the command is in its doc comment). `ZOE_FINISH_DIR` puts the store on another filesystem.
- `crates/zoe-core/tests/finish_visibility.rs` checks N2 with a reader that runs during commits.
- The change: the count read of a commit (the read of each written oid before its first put) costs less. An oid at or after the stored `next_oid` has no record, so the write reads nothing for it. A put whose previous serial is not zero reads that one record first, and scans the oid only when it is gone.

## Where the finish spends its time

The measurement ran on a developer workstation, with two other builders on the same disk. The sync of the database file takes 4 to 16 ms there and varies by a factor of three between runs, so the numbers below come from timers inside the backend and from a store on a memory filesystem, where a sync costs nothing. They are the CPU of the finish, in µs per transaction of 100 records of 1 KB, the mean of 500 transactions, over several runs:

| part | `add`, none | `update`, none | `add`, zstd | `update`, zstd |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| encode the frames (`format::encode_frame_with`) | 17 | 19 | 680 to 740 | 740 to 800 |
| insert into `records` | 155 to 175 | 230 to 310 | 95 to 110 | 170 to 200 |
| insert into `txn_oids` | 45 to 55 | 50 to 56 | 45 to 58 | 53 to 60 |
| the count read, before the change | 41 to 47 | 51 to 61 | 45 to 64 | 55 to 61 |
| the count read, after the change | 6 | 36 to 44 | 7 to 10 | 35 to 49 |
| redb commit, its writes included | 107 to 120 | 288 to 378 | 84 to 107 | 221 to 289 |
| bytes written per finish | 195 KB | 735 KB | 125 KB | 588 KB |

- With zstd, the default, the encoding is more than half of the CPU of the finish. `format/codec.rs` creates one zstd context for each record. A test that kept one context per thread (not committed) brought the encoding from 680 to 740 µs down to 490 to 537 µs, with the same bytes. That code is in the red zone `format/`, and compression is ZOE-172.
- `update` writes 7 times the bytes of its records: each record goes next to the older records of its oid, so each of the 100 records copies its own leaf page of `records`. That is the layout of the keys, ZOE-174.
- The rest of the finish outside the backend (the group commit, the locks, the staging) is under 10 µs.

## What was tried

- The count read (kept): about 40 µs less for `add`, about 20 µs less for `update`.
- Coalesced writes of the database file (not kept): a test file layer that kept the writes until the sync and wrote each run of adjacent pages at once. It brought `add` from 48 writes to 9, and `update` from 171 to 102, and the time of the writes by 25 to 55 µs. That needs a buffer between redb and the file, which every read must see; the gain did not justify that risk.
- The index keys after the records, in a second loop (not kept): no change.
