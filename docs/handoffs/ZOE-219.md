## Deviations from ADR 0048

- `zoe_structural_merges_total` of `zoe serve` counts a node of rule 1 as `merged`: its entry has the outcome `Merged` with `split` 1, and its log line writes `passed`. A new outcome would change the match of `zoe-server`, outside the scope fence.
- In shadow mode, the entry of a stale oid of a node is the entry of tier 1 without the rules (for a node, a refusal of reason 11), with the outcome of the rules in `shadow` and the rule in `split`. A stale read-current node gets an entry of its own: outcome `undecided` ("a read-current oid"), shadow `passed`. The new bucket of the transaction is not stale, so it has no line in shadow mode.
- The range of rule 3 merges without the rules of ADR 0042 (the posting-dict rule and the first-key rule).
- The order of key references applies to the values of a merged range too, not only to its keys. `bucket_merge` compares the values of the keys that both sides kept, and `refs` of the intid utility holds key references as values: without the order, the first split of `refs` in the acceptance test (round 7) gave no answer. BTrees calls the same `object_compare`, so `_get_cmp_keys`, for keys and values. The human approved it, and ADR 0048 records it.

## The review of step 3

- A refusal of rule 3 fails the vote on the stale record of its range (the split bucket, or the root that held its bucket inline), not on a node that did not change.
- Rule 2 merges a split of a child node that the other side did not change; `CLAUDE.md` and ADR 0048 say so. The rules run on each node by itself, at any depth: the code has no check of the depth, and the ADR no longer names one.
- The inline case also takes the last node of a tree that holds its one bucket inline: the comments and `CLAUDE.md` say why no update is lost there. No test: such a tree has no chain of next buckets that the walk of the tests can check.
- `Heap::new_reference` is built only with the feature `testing` of zoe-pickle, which only the dev-dependency of zoe-core turns on.
- Second review: a campaign gives `--split-merges` only to the stacks that take it, and one run still refuses it on another stack; only `run` and `campaign` take the option; the client that writes the policy does not count in `startup_seconds` (`policy_seconds`); a refusal of the split merge takes the mode of its record with the mode of its node. The campaign of step 4 used `run`, not `campaign`, so the stop of a mixed campaign did not touch it.
