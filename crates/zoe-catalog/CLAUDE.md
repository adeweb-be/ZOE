# zoe-catalog

The catalog mirror: the ZCatalog indexes of each registered catalog of a store in memory, answering queries as of a tid (ADR 0059). Specification: "Catalog acceleration and search", "Step 2: the mirror".

Built: FieldIndex, KeywordIndex, DateIndex, DateRangeIndex, BooleanIndex, UUIDIndex, ExtendedPathIndex and the `ZoeSecurityIndex` of `zoe.plone` with their clauses, and the sorts on FieldIndex, DateIndex and UUIDIndex; the order clause, a sort on the `GopipIndex` of plone.folder over one folder (ADR 0059, the amendment of ZOE-302); the registration, the build, the base and its change sets, the rebase, the mirror window, the shadow rebuild of a stale mirror, the memory bound and the counts; the metadata table and the rows of a reply; the plan and the reply of section 8 as a tree of msgpack values (`Wire`, with its one converter from and to rmpv), with the kind of each clause (`decode_typed`); the transaction overlay of a query. `zoe serve` hosts the mirrors of each store and answers `catalog_query` over the protocol (`zoe-server`, `protocol/catalog.rs`). With the feature `text`, the module `text` gives the native text index of each registered catalog (ADR 0060): its settings in `zoe.text`, the tantivy index in `text/<oid>/` and its thread, the enhanced mode, the text clause of a plan, the text overlay, `text_query` and `text_info`. The compatibility mode comes later.

## Layout

- `read.rs`: `Reads`, what the mirror reads of a store (`zoe_core::Store` gives it), the decode of a record and the walk of a tree over several threads.
- `base.rs`, `build.rs`: the rows and the structures of a base, and their build.
- `table.rs`, `cell.rs`: the metadata table of a base, and the msgpack cells of its columns with their ext types.
- `change.rs`: the change sets and the read of the records of a commit.
- `mirror.rs`: the versions of one mirror, the window and the rebase.
- `query.rs`: the clauses of each index type and the four sort paths of ZCatalog.
- `security.rs`: the `ZoeSecurityIndex` of `zoe.plone`: its inputs, their resolution through the paths of the rows, the distinct values of a base, and a version over it.
- `plan.rs`: the codec of a plan and of a reply.
- `order.rs`: the order clause of a plan, and the read of the order of its folder at each query.
- `overlay.rs`: the transaction overlay of a query: its decode from msgpack, provisional oids, and its read over the version of the query.
- `mirrors.rs`: the mirrors of a store, their thread, their builds and the bound.
- `shadow.rs`: the shadow rebuild: the structures that a stale commit changed, their build beside the base, their catch-up and the swap.
- `reason.rs`, `counts.rs`: the reasons of a fallback and the counts.
- `testing.rs`, with the feature `testing`: the records of a catalog as Products.ZCatalog writes them, and `commit`; with the feature `text`, `commit_text`, the deltas and the settings of the text indexes of a catalog.
- `text/`, with the feature `text`: `settings.rs` (the namespace `zoe.text`), `clause.rs` (the text clause, the replies of `text_query` and `text_info`), `analysis.rs`, `index.rs` (the schema, the directory, the maps of the documents), `translate.rs`, `search.rs` (the filter, the integer scores), `overlay.rs` (the text overlay), `texts.rs` (the indexes of a store and their thread, and the build from the live texts that a rebuild uses), `engine.rs` (the answer of a clause), `rebuild.rs` (the rebuild of `zoe catalog rebuild-text` in `text/<oid>.new`, its two renames, and the removal of a `.new` or `.old` directory at the open). `tests/text.rs` is its acceptance test; `tests/shadow_rebuild.rs` is the acceptance test of the shadow rebuild; `tests/data/text.json` keeps the hits and the scores of its corpus, and its ignored test `write_the_corpus_data` writes it.
- `tests/data/zcatalog.py` writes `tests/data/zcatalog.json`: records and answers of Products.ZCatalog on FieldIndexes, for `tests/mirror.rs`. `tests/data/indexes.py` writes `tests/data/indexes.json` for the other index types, for `tests/indexes.rs`. `tests/data/path.py` writes `tests/data/path.json` for ExtendedPathIndex, and with `multi-valued` `tests/data/path-multi.json`, for `tests/path.rs`. `tests/data/order.py` writes `tests/data/order.json`: folders of plone.folder with the records of their orders and positions, and the answers of ZCatalog to listings sorted on the `GopipIndex`, for `tests/order.rs`. `tests/data/metadata.py` writes `tests/data/metadata.json`: records of `data` with the columns of a Plone 6.1 catalog and the brains of ZCatalog, for `tests/metadata.rs`. Run each with `PYTHONHASHSEED=0`.

## Invariants

- A query is answered as of the snapshot tid of the client, exclusive as the tid of `loadBefore`. A result never names an object that the Connection cannot load.
- A query with an index or an option that the mirror does not serve takes the Python path, whole, with its reason. So does a snapshot outside the mirror window, and a catalog that is not registered, not ready, refused, dropped or stale. The names of the reasons are a contract (`Reason::name`). The store also gives `sort`, a reason of the client in ADR 0059, section 7, for several sort indexes on the path of ZCatalog that iterates the first sort index: only the size of the result decides that path. A range bound that the keys of the index do not order gives `option`.
- Rids, order, `len()` and `actual_result_count` equal those of ZCatalog, ties included: each of the four sort paths keeps its own rule of ties, and a batch past the middle takes the other end.
- Security stays in Plone: a `KeywordIndex` `allowedRolesAndUsers` is an index like any other, and a `ZoeSecurityIndex` resolves as its Python path does (ADR 0061). It reads `_inputs` in the place of `_unindex`. A change set holds the changed inputs of a row, never a resolved value. A query resolves again, with the inputs of its version, the rows whose inputs or path the overrides changed and the rows of the base below their old and new paths; the rebase counts those rows. A range or `not` on it gives `option`.
- Each index reads the record of the index object, its `_unindex` tree and its `_length`, and no other tree: no `_index`, no `_index_value` of a BooleanIndex, no `_since` or `_always` of a DateRangeIndex, no `_index_parents` or `_index_items` of an ExtendedPathIndex. An ExtendedPathIndex reads `_depth` and `multi_valued` from its record. FieldIndex, DateIndex, UUIDIndex and BooleanIndex keep the structure of a FieldIndex; a KeywordIndex keeps the keys of each row; a DateRangeIndex keeps `since` and `until` of each row and the rows sorted by each. A change set keeps the value of a row of a KeywordIndex as its distinct keys in ascending order, and a row without a key as a row without a value.
- A UUIDIndex whose `_length` in the version differs from its rows with a UUID gives `index_unavailable` to each clause and sort on it. `_length` counts the keys of `_index`, which the mirror does not read. A UUID at two rows, or a UUID that left `_index` while a row keeps it (`removeForwardIndexEntry` deletes the key whichever row holds it), makes ZCatalog find other rows than `_unindex` gives. The count follows the `_length` and the rows of each change set.
- A key of `not` that the keys of the index do not order gives `option`: ZCatalog raises.
- A path clause follows `ExtendedPathIndex.search` step by step, with the `None` of BTrees: `intersection` with no set gives the other set whole, so a level at which no row ends adds the whole set of the path. A search that reads `_index_items` (the level 0 with a depth of 0, a `navtree` of depth 0, and a `navtree` of depth 1 with `multi_valued`) gives `index_unavailable`: `_index_items` keeps one rid for each path, loses it when any row of the path leaves, and keeps the key of a path of one component, so no tree that the mirror reads gives it. A commit that raises `_depth` writes the index record, so the mirror goes stale and builds again.
- A rebase gives a new row only to a rid that the old base has no row for, whatever the order of the new rids.
- The build reads `data` on a heap that holds the objects of other classes, so a value of another type is a cell of another type, not a refusal. A record of `data` that the decoder refuses makes the catalog `refused`. The table keeps one column for each position of the `schema` of the catalog record, and nothing when the schema does not give each position from 0 once.
- The cells that the table models: nil, bool, an int of 64 bits, float, str, bytes, a tuple, a dict, `Missing.Value` (ext 1), a `DateTime` of NEWOBJ with no arguments and the state `(int, bool, str)` (ext 2), a `Message` of REDUCE (ext 3), each nested at most 32 deep. A cell of another type (a list, a set, an instance of another class, a persistent reference, a class, a larger int) never goes into a row: the row comes as nil, and the client makes the brain with the stock `Catalog.__getitem__`.
- A brain made from a row equals the brain that `Catalog.__getitem__` makes from `data` at the snapshot: the same values and types after the round trip into Python, the same `getRID()`, `data_record_id_` and `getPath()`. So a row comes only for a record with one value for each column of the schema (the fast path of `Catalog.instantiate`), and a column that the schema does not name makes each row nil.
- A change set holds, for a record of `data`, one change for each changed cell (`Slot::Column`) and one for the length of the record (`Slot::Width`) when it changed. A record of `data` applies through the same code for a commit and for a transaction overlay.
- A reply holds rows and paths for its first `rn` rids only, and only when the plan names `cols`.
- The order clause (`o`) sorts on the `GopipIndex` of the plan alone. The mirror reads the order (`persistent.list.PersistentList`) only at the oid of the clause, before the tid of the plan, or from the overlay of the query, at each query, and keeps nothing of it. It gives the place of each row as `documentToKeyMap` does for one folder: the id from the path of the row in the version, a later rid over an earlier one with the same id, a later place over an earlier one, no place for the rid 0, and no place for an item that is not a str. A row whose parent is not the path of the clause, and a sort index that is not a `plone.folder.nogopip.GopipIndex` in the version, give `sort`; an order that does not load or decode gives `index_unavailable`, or `overlay` from the overlay. The places are distinct, so the paths of `sortResults` give the rows by place up to the limit; the path that iterates the index never runs. `decode_typed` refuses a plan with `o` (`Unknown`), so a reader without the order clause gives `plan`.
- A transaction overlay serves one query. The mirror reads it with `read_records`, as a commit, over the version at the tid of the query, into overrides of its own; it never enters the change sets or the shapes, and no other query sees it. A new object of the overlay has a provisional oid (the high bit set). A record that does not read, a new pickle of the catalog object or of a mirrored index, and a node of the overlay whose shape a commit after the tid of the query changed give `overlay`: the shapes are those of the last commit applied, and `Maint::moved` keeps the tid of the last change of the shape of each node. The thread lays the shapes of a commit before its change set, so a query never reads a version with older shapes. `moved` forgets the changes at or before the floor of the versions (`Maint::pruned`), so a query whose tid is below `pruned` when it reads the shapes gives `overlay`. `read_records` reads each record once (a queue, with the places of each oid), so a crafted order costs no more than a commit. The bound counts the overlays of the queries that run (two copies of each pickle and the copy of the overrides): an overlay that does not fit gives `overlay` and drops no mirror (`Shared::fits`), and the drops of a build, a rebase or a growth do not count the overlays.
- A host of the mirrors can make the counters before the mirrors start (`Mirrors::start_counting`), so a query that it refuses before (`not_registered`, `plan`) counts too: `zoe serve` does.
- A clause keeps its kind on the wire: `v` reads a FieldIndex, a KeywordIndex, a DateIndex, a UUIDIndex or a `ZoeSecurityIndex`, `b` a BooleanIndex, `d` a DateRangeIndex and `p` an ExtendedPathIndex (`ClauseKind::reads`). `Mirrors::query_wire` refuses a clause of another kind with `plan` (`View::check_kinds`), before the class of the index reads the clause. A clause on an index that the mirror does not serve passes the check and gets its own reason.
- The byte arenas of the table hold at most 4 GiB: past it, the build refuses the catalog, and a rebase fails. The walk of `data` counts its cells against `catalog-memory` as it collects them.
- A clause on a DateRangeIndex applies after the other clauses. A row of the result without an entry in a DateRangeIndex of the query gives `date_range_order`, as does a date outside the range of the index with another clause: the answer of ZCatalog then depends on the order of its plan. The only clause of a query comes first in that plan.
- The mirror builds only registered catalogs: the keys of the durable namespace `zoe.catalog`, read at the start and after each commit that changes its generation. No scan of the store, no build on a first query.
- A base never changes. A change set holds only the changed values. A rebase keeps the number of each row, so the structures that it does not build again stay shared.
- The mirrors of a store stay within `catalog-memory`, the memory of a build, of a rebase and of a shadow rebuild included. Past it, the mirror that answered a query least recently drops, and the drop is counted.
- The publication of a commit never waits for the mirror: `Mirrors::committed` only records the tid. The mirror never makes a commit fail. A mirror that fails (a panic, a refused record) gives the Python path to its catalog, logs once through `take_events`, and never gives a wrong result.
- A commit that writes another pickle for the catalog object or for a mirrored index object, deletes a mirrored object, writes a node that the change sets cannot follow, or writes a new record of an unavailable index (the index object, or the record that the build refused: `Owner::Unavailable`), makes the mirror stale. The read of the commit gives what it did not follow (`Read::unfollowed`), and `shadow::changed_by` gives the changed structures: the rows (a new `paths` or `uids`, or a catalog object that does not read), the table (a new `data` or schema), and the indexes by id (a new index object, an added or a removed index, a tree that does not follow). A new pickle of the catalog object that changes none of these changes nothing. The change set of that commit and of each later one loses the changes of the changed structures (`shadow::strip`).
- A stale mirror keeps its base and starts a shadow rebuild in a thread of its own (`zoe-catalog-shadow`), under the one-build lock of the process, as of the tid of the last stale commit. It builds the changed structures one at a time over the rows of the version at that tid, takes the other structures from a rebase of that version, then applies the later commits to the changed structures. A commit there that it cannot follow starts it again at that tid. No rebase runs while a shadow rebuild is due or runs. The thread of the mirrors applies the last commits to the shadow and lays it in at one step, with new versions and a new `Maint`: a query never sees a mix of the old and the new structures, and the old base stays until its last query ends.
- During a shadow rebuild, a query at the tid of a stale commit or later that reads a changed structure (`Changed::needed_by`: an index of a clause or of the sort, the rows, the table for a reply with `cols`) takes `stale`; every other query stays native. After the swap, a query before the stale commit that reads a changed structure takes `outside_window`, until the floor passes the stale commit. The state of the mirror is `building` while a shadow rebuild is due or runs. A commit that changes the rows starts no shadow rebuild: a second whole base does not fit the bound, so the mirror drops its base and builds again from nothing (`make_stale`). A shadow rebuild that fails (a panic in its thread, in the last catch-up or in the swap, a refused catalog, no room) does the same (`fall_back`), as of its first stale commit, and logs once. A removed registration cancels its shadow rebuild. A new record of a refused index whose shadow rebuild is due or runs marks nothing (`Unfollowed::Refused`), and a shadow rebuild that refuses the index again watches only its index object, so a refused record that each commit rewrites starts at most one rebuild. The bound counts the bytes that a shadow rebuild holds as the bytes of its mirror (`Entry::held`), and a shadow rebuild that would pass it drops the mirror that answered least recently.
- The crate is synchronous: no tokio, no Python inside. It starts its threads in `Mirrors::start`, never at the load of a module. A build takes a share of the store for each read, so it never keeps the store open; `stop` waits for the thread and the builds.
- One build at a time in a process. A build decodes the keys straight into arenas, freed whole when the structure is built.
- No mutex of the mirror is held while a query computes: a query takes the base and the change sets that it needs, then releases the lock.
- The text index is a cache: the deltas of the store are the source of truth, and its loss costs a rebuild and loses nothing. A text hit never names a rid that the snapshot does not hold: in a plan, the text clause searches inside the rows of the version. The matched set at a snapshot that a text clause serves is the set of the live texts at that snapshot, also with the index behind or ahead (the text overlay). The staged texts of a query serve that query only. A damaged delta stops the index of its catalog: each text query of the catalog then gives `corrupt`, never a guessed text.
- Only the process that holds the lock of the store writes `text/`: a read-only open (`TextConfig::read_only`) opens a directory that refuses each write. The index never makes a commit fail or wait: `Mirrors::committed` only records the tid for its thread too. The index holds a cap of pack at its tid, and rebuilds when the pack watermark passes the tid of its cap. The threads of tantivy start with the text index of a catalog with settings, never at import. A build without the feature `text` links no tantivy.
- On the Python path, a `ZoeTextIndex` asks the text index with `text_query`: a query that takes the Python path, whole, and a snapshot older than the mirror window, still get the native text index.
