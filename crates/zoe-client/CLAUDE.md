# zoe-client

A Rust client for the ZEO protocol, with the ZOE extensions. `zoe.zeo.ClientStorage`, `zoe-bench`, the server mode of Loupe, `zoe mcp` against a server and the simulator use it. Specification: "Async and free-threading", F2 (client side), F28 and N18, and "The client cache" under "The native client (F28)".

## Layout

- `src/client.rs`: `Client`, `Options`, `Transaction`, `Invalidations`, the open on several endpoints (`Client::open_first`), and the three tasks of a connection (reader, writer, heartbeat).
- `src/pool.rs`: `Pool`, `PoolTransaction`, `Finished`, `Turn`, `Delivery`, `Event`, `Reconnect`, `Verification`: several transactions at once on a pool of connections, with one listener and one dispatcher (ADR 0036), and the reconnect with the verification of the cache.
- `src/cache.rs`: `Cache` and `CacheCounts`, the client cache in the layout of ADR 0034: stage 1, one anonymous mapping (`Cache::new`), and stage 2, a file that one process maps (`Cache::open`, in `src/cache/file.rs`). `src/cache/mapping.rs` holds the mapping. `src/cache/tests.rs` holds its unit tests.
- `src/endpoint.rs`: `Address` (TCP or the path of a unix domain socket) and `Endpoint` (an address, with TLS or without).
- `src/tls.rs`: `TlsOptions` and `Tls`, the TLS of a client on rustls.
- `src/protocol/`: the frames and the messages of ZEO 6 with msgpack. It has its own codec: the codec of `zoe-server` is private to that crate.
- `src/runtime.rs`: the trait `Runtime` (connect, connect_unix, spawn, sleep) and `Tokio`, its implementation for production.
- `src/error.rs`: `Error`.

## Invariants

- The client speaks the same ZEO 6 msgpack protocol as `ZEO.ClientStorage`: `M5`, then `register(storage, read_only)`, then calls with int ids from 1 and the flags `false`, and calls without a reply with the id 0 and the flags `true`. `loadBefore` has an int id too, not the `[oid, tid]` id of a stock client: the server echoes any id. `Client::connect` registers with `false` and two arguments.
- `Client::open` declares `Options::extensions` in a third argument of `register`, then calls `get_info`. A stock server refuses the third argument with `TypeError` (any module, such as `builtins.TypeError`) and keeps the connection open and unregistered: the client then registers again with two arguments. With no extension in `Options`, `register` has two arguments. `Client::info` gives the reply of `get_info`, and `Client::extensions` the names of its key `extensions` that the client declared.
- The client uses an extension only after the server accepted it in `get_info`. `sequence_next` needs `sequences`: without it, it fails with `Error::Unsupported` and sends nothing.
- `traced(traceparent, future)` gives a W3C traceparent to the calls of `future`, through a task-local of tokio. On a connection whose server accepted `trace_context` in `get_info`, each call and each call without a reply inside it carries the traceparent as a fifth element of its message: the map `{"traceparent": traceparent}`, the wire form of `zoe serve`. A call outside `traced`, a call with `None`, a call on another connection, the heartbeat and the abort of a dropped `Transaction` have four elements. So do the calls of the open (`register`, the read-only `register` of the fallback, `lastTransaction` and `get_info`), in `open`, `open_endpoint` and `open_first`: the flag of the connection comes from its own `get_info`, after them, so `open_first` keeps the flag of the server that it keeps. The caller reads the traceparent before it starts the future, so a call that waits keeps the traceparent of its caller.
- A call frame is built on the task of the caller, inside its `traced`. A task that the client spawns sees no traceparent, so a call that it sends has four elements.
- Each reply goes to its call by its message id, whatever the order of the replies. A reply with an id that no call waits for is dropped.
- The reader reads each frame with `protocol::read_payload`. A payload shorter than 16 KiB is one read into one buffer. A longer payload reads its first 64 bytes first: when they are the head of a reply that `decode` gives without an error, whose args are a bin or an array that starts with a bin, the bytes of the bin go from the stream into a buffer of their own, and `decode_payload` gives that buffer as the `Value::Binary` of the reply. A rest of at most 64 bytes after the bin comes in the same read. Each other payload is one buffer, which `decode` reads. Both paths have the same depth limit for the items after the bin. So the data of a large `loadBefore` or `loadSerial` reply is copied once from the socket, and `Client::load_before_at` and `load_serial` move it into the `Record` or the `Vec` without a copy. The cold load of a record takes three copies of its data: from the socket, into the cache, and into the `bytes` of the binding.
- `recycle(buffer)` gives the buffer of a bin back after its use; the next bin of at least 16 KiB takes the smallest kept buffer that holds it, and a shorter bin never takes one. It keeps at most 16 buffers of 16 KiB to 256 KiB; when it holds 16, a larger buffer takes the place of the smallest one. Its lock is only tried, never awaited: while another thread holds it, the buffer drops or the bin gets a new one. So a caller with the GIL never waits on it, and a child of `fork` never waits for ever. Without the reuse, glibc gives the pages of a freed buffer back to the kernel, and the next buffer faults them in again.
- A lost connection fails every call that waits with `Error::Disconnected`, and every later call too. The end of the stream, an error of the stream, a frame that does not decode, an `invalidateTransaction` in a bad shape, `Client::close` and the drop of the `Client` all close the connection.
- The client reaches the network, its tasks and the time only through `Runtime`: never `tokio::net`, `tokio::spawn` or `tokio::time` outside `Tokio`. It uses tokio's I/O traits and `tokio::sync`. Every `select!` is `biased;`, so a simulator runs it in one order. `Runtime::connect_unix` has a default that fails with `io::ErrorKind::Unsupported`, so a runtime without unix sockets, such as the simulator of `zoe-server`, needs no change.
- TLS runs on the stream that `Runtime` gives, with rustls and its ring provider: the TLS stack of `zoe-server`. `Tls::load` reads the PEM files of `TlsOptions`: the certificate chain of the client, its key (in the certificate file when no key file is set) and the CA certificates of `authenticate`. `TlsOptions::without_certificate` gives a client without a certificate, as ZEO's client without `certificate`: the load reads no key, ignores `key`, and the handshake sends no client certificate. `authenticate` is a file, whose every certificate must fit, or a directory, whose every file the client reads. `TlsOptions::system` has no `authenticate`: the client then takes the CA certificates of the system from `rustls-native-certs` (`SSL_CERT_FILE` and `SSL_CERT_DIR` when one is set, else the default paths of OpenSSL), as the default context of Python's `ssl` does. In a directory and in the system store, a certificate or a file that does not read is skipped, and no CA certificate at all fails the load. `rustls-native-certs` is only in this crate. The client always checks the chain of the server certificate against these CA certificates. With `check_hostname`, the webpki verifier of rustls checks the chain and the server name of the `Endpoint`. Without it, the verifier `ChainOnly` calls `verify_server_cert_signed_by_trust_anchor` of rustls and never checks the name: the security of the chain never depends on the order of the checks inside rustls. An error names the file.
- Known limits: in a directory, the client reads every file, while OpenSSL reads only the hashed names of `openssl rehash`. The server certificate must chain to a CA certificate of `authenticate`. A self-signed server certificate (a CA certificate or a v1 certificate, as the `server.pem` of ZEO's tests) given as `authenticate` is refused by webpki, while OpenSSL accepts it. `zoe serve` matches such a client certificate by its bytes; the client does not do it yet.
- An `Endpoint` with TLS never sends a frame outside TLS: a failed handshake fails the connection, and the client never falls back to a stream without TLS.
- `Client::open_first` opens the endpoints at once, in the task of the caller, and keeps one: the first that registers as `Options` asks. With `Options::read_only_fallback`, a server that refuses the writable `register` with `ReadOnlyError` gets a second `register`, read-only, on the same connection; that client waits until no other endpoint can open writable, or until the wait passes. The drop of the other attempts and clients closes their connections, so one connection stays open. Without a client, the open fails with the most specific error: the first error that is not of the connection (`Io`, `Disconnected`), else the last `Io` of the kind `InvalidData` (a failed TLS handshake or a bad stream), else the last error of a connection, or `Io` of the kind `TimedOut` when the wait passed first and no attempt failed. So a refused address never hides a certificate error of another address. A read-only fallback waits for the slowest attempt: an address that never answers delays it until the end of the wait. `Client::read_only` tells if the client registered read-only, and `Client::address` gives the address of its server.
- The client sends a heartbeat `[-1, 0, ".reply", nil]` every 60 seconds (`HEARTBEAT`), busy or idle, as a stock client does. So the `--idle-timeout` of `zoe serve` never closes it.
- One transaction at a time for each client, as the commit lock of `ZEO.ClientStorage`. A vote that fails, by an error or by a conflict that the vote lists to `vote` or `vote_resolved`, sends `tpc_abort` and waits for its reply, because the server keeps the locks of a listed conflict. `vote_listed` leaves a listed conflict to its caller. A `Transaction` dropped before the server confirmed `finish` or `abort` sends `tpc_abort` and does not wait, so a cancelled `vote` or `abort` still releases the locks. It keeps the commit lock of the client until its `tpc_abort` is in the outgoing channel, also when the channel is full, so the `tpc_begin` of the next transaction never reaches the server before that abort. After a failed vote or an abort, `store`, `vote` and `finish` fail with `Error::Invalid` and send nothing: `zoe serve` closes the connection on a `storea` for a transaction that is not open.
- `Transaction::store_blob` uploads a blob as `ZEO.ClientStorage` does without a shared blob directory: `storea`, `storeBlobStart`, one `storeBlobChunk` per chunk of `BLOB_CHUNK` (59,000) bytes, and `storeBlobEnd`, all without a reply. `store_blob_from` does the same from an `io::Read`, one full chunk in memory at a time; a failed read gives `Error::Io`, sends no `storeBlobEnd` and ends the transaction as a failed vote does: a later `store`, `vote` or `finish` fails with `Error::Invalid` and sends nothing, and the abort or the drop sends `tpc_abort`. `store_blob_shared` sends `storea` and `storeBlobShared(oid, serial, data, filename, id)`, without a reply, for a blob directory that the client shares with the server.
- `Client::send_blob(oid, serial, sink)` downloads a blob into an `io::Write`: `sendBlob`, which the server answers with `receiveBlobStart`, the `receiveBlobChunk`s and `receiveBlobStop` on the same connection, then the reply. The reader gives each of these calls to the download of its oid and serial through a bounded channel (`PIECES`), and waits while it is full; a call for a blob that no download waits for goes nowhere, and one in a bad shape closes the connection. The download succeeds only with the reply, `receiveBlobStart` first and `receiveBlobStop`; a piece out of order or a reply before the stop is `Error::Protocol`, an error reply is the error of the server, and a failed write of the sink is `Error::Io` after the reply, with no write after it. The writes run on the task of the caller. A connection runs one download of a blob at a time: a second `send_blob` of the same oid and serial waits until the reply of the first, also when the future of the first dropped after its `sendBlob` went out; the reader frees the blob at that reply, or at the close. The caller polls a download until it ends, or drops it: while its channel is full, the other replies and the invalidations of its connection wait. A reply scans the downloads for its id only when a download runs. `Pool::send_blob` takes a connection other than the listener, in turn, when one is open and not lost, so the delivery of the invalidations never waits for a download; else the listener.
- The client runs no resolution code. A `ConflictError` is `Error::Conflict`. A conflict that the vote lists is `Error::Conflict` for `vote` and `vote_resolved`, which then abort. A `ReadConflictError` with an oid, as the vote gives after `Transaction::check_current_serial`, is `Error::ReadConflict`. Every other error of the server is `Error::Server` with its class; its message is empty without an argument, the text of its one argument, or else the value.
- `Client::begin` sends `tpc_begin` with the tid nil and the status `" "`. `Client::begin_with` sends a tid and a status: a transaction with a tid commits under it.
- `Transaction::vote_resolved` gives the oids that a stock server lists as resolved: their pickle on the server is not the pickle of the client. `vote` drops them.
- `Client::call(method, args)` and `Client::notify(method, args)` send any call of the server, with a reply and without one, as `_call` and `_async` of `ZEO.ClientStorage`; `Client::ping` calls `ping`. An error reply is `Error::Server` with `value`, the args or the attributes as the server sent them, so a bytes argument (the oid of a `POSKeyError`) stays bytes. `Transaction::delete_object`, `restore` and `undo` send `deleteObject`, `restorea` (nil data for a deletion) and `undoa`, without a reply, with the checks of `store`.
- With `Options::client_label`, `Client::open` sends `set_client_label(label)` without a reply after `get_info`, as ZEO sends it in `notify_connected`. The reader merges the map of each `info` call of the server into `Client::pushed_info`, with a stamp from one counter of the process: a higher stamp is a later call, on any client. An `info` in a bad shape changes nothing.
- `Client::get_invalidations(tid)` calls `getInvalidations`: `None` for a nil reply, else the last tid of the server and the oids of the commits after `tid`.
- `Transaction::vote_listed` gives a `Voted`: the oids that the server resolved and each `Listed` conflict (`oid`, `committed`, `read`, `data`, the pickle that the client stored), both in the order of the reply. A listed conflict does not abort: the transaction stays open, and the server keeps its locks. The caller stores each resolution over `committed` and votes again, or aborts. A map of the reply without both serials or without bytes as `data` is `Error::Protocol`, and the vote aborts. The caller of `PoolTransaction` stores a resolution with `store_resolved`, and calls `unresolved(oid)` for a conflict that it does not resolve.
- `Client::disconnect` closes the connection without waiting for its tasks, so a caller that does not own the `Client` can close it. `Client::is_connected` is false once the connection closed.
- `last_transaction` only goes up: it takes the maximum of the tid of `register`, of each invalidation and of each commit of the client. It is the position of the connection: the last tid that it read from its own stream. The reader raises it at the reply of a `tpc_finish`, and at each invalidation, before it gives them on. `Client::load_before_at` returns the position that the reader read when the reply of the load came, for the cache of ADR 0034.
- The invalidations go to a broadcast channel of 1,024 entries. A receiver that falls further behind gets `Error::Lagged`, and must treat its cache as stale. A consumer subscribes first, then reads `last_transaction`: the reader raises `last_transaction` before it sends the invalidation. A commit of the client gives no invalidation to the client, as from ZEO. `Invalidations::try_recv` reads without a wait. The reader dispatches the frames in order, so when the reply of `tpc_finish` comes back, each invalidation that the server sent before it is in the channel.
- `Pool` (ADR 0036) holds one open `Client`, the listener, which never commits (a pool of `with_reconnect` holds none while it is disconnected), and opens the other connections with its `Connect` function, on demand, up to its bound. `begin_with` takes a free connection, opens one when none is free and the pool is below its bound, else waits. The connection goes back to the pool when the `PoolTransaction` ends; a lost connection closes and leaves the pool, also when it is lost while free: `begin_with` closes it and takes the next one. A slot of the bound comes back when a connect fails or its future drops. Loads use any connection that is not lost, the listener included, in turn; `new_oids` and `sequence_next` use the listener. `Pool::call` and `Pool::notify` use the listener, so the state that a call keeps on the server, as an iterator, is there for the calls that follow; they refuse a call of a transaction, `register` and `sendBlob` with `Error::Invalid` and send nothing, so the listener never commits. `Pool::info` gives the map of `get_info` of the listener with the map of the newest `info` call of any connection of the pool over it; `Shared::forget` keeps the map of a connection that leaves (`Shared::pushed`), so the sizes never go back, and a new listener empties it.
- `Pool::prefetch(oids, tid)` spawns one load for each oid that the cache does not hold for `tid` (once for an oid that comes twice) and returns their number at once; each load goes through `load_before`, so the store rule of the cache keeps out a current reply that an invalidation ended while it was on its way. A failed load changes nothing. Without a cache it spawns nothing; while the pool has no listener, or after the close, it fails with `Error::Disconnected`. The loads carry no traceparent: the pool spawns them.
- `Pool::sync(wait)` pings the listener; with `wait`, it then waits until the last tid of the pool reaches the position of the listener at the reply, or the pool closes, loses its listener or moves to another generation. A caller that runs a delivery or holds a `Turn` must pass `false`: the wait would wait for itself.
- One dispatcher task of the pool reads the invalidations of the listener and delivers each tid in turn, one at a time. A tid of another client goes to the consumer: `next_delivery` gives it, and `delivered` confirms it. A tid that a finish of the pool committed gives the turn to that finish: `Finished::turn` returns a `Turn`, and its drop confirms the tid. The dispatcher delivers no other tid until the confirmation. `Pool::last_transaction` is the last confirmed tid, from the last tid of the listener at `Pool::new` on, never `Client::last_transaction`. `settled_transaction` waits first for the delivery that runs. A tid at or below the start is skipped.
- The hold: a `PoolTransaction::finish` counts as in flight from before its frame goes out until its reply. The dispatcher takes a horizon when it takes a tid, and holds the tid until no finish of a lower count is in flight, or until a finish names the tid. A finish counted later commits a later tid. A finish that fails, whose connection is lost, or whose future drops, leaves the hold through the drop of its guard. A finish with its reply disarms the guard; nothing is forgotten, so the drop of the pool and the end of the dispatcher free the shared state. A lost listener (`Client::closed`) ends the hold at once. A `Finished` that drops before its turn leaves its tid to the consumer as a tid of another client.
- A lost or lagged listener is a lost connection: the dispatcher disconnects it, fails each finish that waits for its reply, gives the consumer `Delivery::Stale`, then gives each finish that has its reply its turn in tid order, with `Turn::stale`. Then `begin_with` and `finish` fail with `Error::Disconnected` and send nothing. A pool of `Pool::new` or `Pool::with_cache` stays so. A pool needs a consumer of its deliveries: the dispatcher waits for each one. The state of the pool is one `std::sync::Mutex`, held for short sections that never wait, with a `watch` channel that wakes the waiters. The listener and the connections that commit are in an `RwLock`, which a load takes to pick a connection. A writer that changes the listener, or that adds a connection under a generation, takes the mutex of the state first; `forget` removes a connection with the `RwLock` alone. No code takes the mutex of the state while it holds the `RwLock`.
- The reconnect. A pool of `Pool::with_reconnect` takes a `Reconnect`: an `Open` function that opens a listener and gives the `Connect` of its server (with `true`, a writable listener only), `poll`, `refused_poll` (`REFUSED_POLL`, 9 seconds, ZEO's `register_failed_poll`) and `upgrade`. At the loss, the dispatcher also closes and forgets every connection of the server and moves `State::generation`: a `Lease` or an `Opening` of another generation never goes back to the pool, and its `finish` fails with `Error::Disconnected`, so a transaction that began before the loss never commits on a later connection. Then the dispatcher calls `Open` at once, then every `poll`, until the pool closes; a pending listener of the upgrade comes first. The pool never blocks a caller for it: while it has no listener, each call fails with `Error::Disconnected`. Without a first listener, the pool starts so, and its first listener needs no verification.
- The verification follows `verify` of `ZEO/asyncio/client.py`. The dispatcher subscribes to the new listener first, then reads its last tid. The base is `Cache::applied`, or the last tid of the pool without a cache. A server tid equal to the base keeps the cache (`Verification::Kept`). A server tid below the base or below the last tid of the pool refuses the server: `Event::Refused`, the connection closes, and the next attempt waits `refused_poll`. A higher one calls `Client::get_invalidations(base)`: a reply `(tid, oids)` calls `Cache::verify(tid, Some(oids))`, which drops the current entry of each oid and moves `applied` in one hold of the writer lock, then always offers `Delivery::Invalidate` at that tid (`Verification::Quick`), as ZEO always calls `invalidateTransaction`. The tid can be the last tid of the pool: a turn after the loss moved the last tid past `applied`, and the commits of other clients between `applied` and that tid never reached the consumer. A consumer that read an object of such a commit from the cache after `Delivery::Stale` needs these oids, so the offer comes also then, and its confirmation keeps the last tid (`delivered` moves the last tid only up); `None` calls `Cache::verify(server tid, None)`, which drops every entry, and the confirmation of `Event::Connected(Verification::Dropped)` moves the last tid. The confirmation of the consumer moves the last tid, and `deliver` skips each tid at or below it, so the consumer never gets a tid twice. Then the pool takes the server (`State::lost` false, the listener and its `Connect` in place) and offers `Event::Connected`. A verification that fails (a lost connection, an error of `getInvalidations`) closes the connection and tries again.
- `Pool::next_event` gives each event: a `Delivery`, `Connected` or `Refused`. `next_delivery` confirms the other events itself, so a consumer of deliveries only (the simulator) needs no change. Each offer carries the tid that its confirmation moves the last tid to.
- The upgrade: with `Reconnect::upgrade`, a task tries `Open(true)` every `poll` while the listener is read-only. A writable listener goes to `State::pending`, and the task closes the read-only listener: the dispatcher then takes the pending listener as after a loss. The task stores it only when, under the mutex of the state, the pool is not lost and its listener is the one that the task saw before the open; else it closes the writable client and tries again. The dispatcher skips a pending listener that is no longer connected.
- The client cache (ADR 0034). `Pool::with_cache` gives the pool a `Cache`; `Pool::new` gives none. The semantics are those of `ZEO.cache.ClientCache`. An entry is one revision of an oid with its start tid and its end tid; a current entry has the end word 0, and a dropped entry has its end equal to its start, so it serves no snapshot. `load_before_with(oid, before)` hits an entry with `start < before` and either `end ≥ before`, or no end and `before ≤ applied + 1`. `load_with(oid)` hits the current entry whatever `applied` is, as `ZEO.ClientStorage.load` does. A lookup follows at most 8 entries of an oid (`MAX_CHAIN`), and an index insert probes at most 32 slots (`MAX_PROBE`); an entry that no slot takes counts in `CacheCounts::unindexed`, and no lookup finds it.
- The layout is ADR 0034: a header page of 512 words (the words 0 to 3: magic `ZOECACH` with the version of the layout in its last byte, size of the ring, number of slots, and the static checksum; 4 and 5: the boot id of the kernel; 6: the clean flag; 7: `applied`; 8: `head`; 9: the state checksum, the xxh3 of the words 0 to 8; 10: the length of the name of the storage, at most 256 bytes; 11 to 42: its bytes. The static checksum is the xxh3 of the magic, the size, the number of slots and the words 10 to 42. An anonymous mapping has an empty name, and leaves the boot id, the clean flag and the state checksum at zero), an index of `max(ring ÷ 256, 1024)` slots rounded up to a power of two, each `(oid + 1, position)`, and a ring of `cache_size` bytes rounded down to a word. An entry has a head of 9 words (its logical position, oid, start, end, the position of the older entry of the oid, stored and raw lengths, codec and total bytes, the xxh3 of its data, the xxh3 of the first 8 words) and its data, aligned to 8 bytes. An entry never crosses the end of the ring. Logical positions start at one ring and never repeat. The same code must serve the file of a later stage: do not write a second layout.
- Every word of the mapping is an `AtomicU64`. A hit takes no lock and writes nothing: it loads `applied` (acquire), the slot (acquire), copies the entry with relaxed loads in one pass, 4 KiB at a time into a local buffer that it hashes (streaming xxh3) and then gives to the caller's sink (`Copier`), checks both checksums, then an acquire fence and a load of `head`: the entry is intact while `head ≤ position + ring`. A writer moves `head`, then a release fence, then writes the entry with relaxed stores, then points the slot at it with release stores. The store of an entry writes its data and hashes it in one pass, 4 KiB at a time, then writes its head with the checksum. The end of an entry changes in place: the end word, then the head checksum (release). A doubt is a miss.
- The writers (`store`, `apply`, `commit`, `drop_current`, `clear`, `disable`, and the copy of a hit) take one `std::sync::Mutex`, never across an `await`. A hit of a current entry in the oldest fifth of the ring, no larger than a tenth of the ring, copies it to the head only when `try_lock` gets the mutex. An entry larger than half of the ring is never cached (`Cache::fits`).
- The store rule: `Cache::store` takes a current reply only when `applied` is not above `seen`, the position of the connection that carried it, which the reader took when the reply arrived (`Client::load_before_at`). A reply with an end always goes in. A newer current reply drops the older current entry of its oid: the older revision ended somewhere up to the new start, not surely at it. An older current reply than a current entry is refused. `store` returns true only when an index slot took the entry, so a lookup can find it. The oid `u64::MAX` is never cached: its key would be 0, the key of an empty slot. `Pool::load_before` stores each reply with its position, and `Pool::load` goes through it. Neither reads the cache: the caller looks up `Pool::cache` first.
- The order: the dispatcher calls `Cache::apply(tid, oids)` before it offers a tid of another client to the consumer, a dropped `Finished` included. `Finished::turn` calls `Cache::commit(tid, writes)` when its turn comes, before it returns the `Turn`: each write ends the current entry of its oid at the tid, and adds the committed data, unless the server resolved the oid (`vote_resolved`, `vote_listed`) or the data does not fit. The pickle of `store_resolved`, which the server stores unchanged, is a write like any other, as in ZEO. `PoolTransaction` keeps its stores only when the pool has a cache, and the turn applies the last store of each oid. `delete_object` and `restore` keep a write without data: the turn ends the current entry of the oid and adds nothing. An oid that the vote lists as resolved by the server and that the transaction did not store, as an oid of an undo, becomes a write without data too, as `TransactionBuffer` of ZEO gives it. A `Conflict` or a `ReadConflict` of a vote, and `PoolTransaction::unresolved(oid)`, call `Cache::drop_current(oid)`. A listed conflict alone changes nothing in the cache. `Pool::with_cache` moves `applied` of the empty cache to the last tid of the listener.
- `Error::OtherProtocol` is a server that answers the handshake with another protocol than `M5` (`Z4`, `Z5`). The client sends nothing. `Client::open_first` ranks it with a failed TLS handshake, and `zoe-py` counts it as a failed connection.
- `Reconnect::dropping`, a `Dropping`, runs before a verification drops every entry of the cache (`getInvalidations` gives `None` or fails), at a reconnect and at the verification of a file, with the reason; the pool waits for its future, or the close, then drops. So `zoe.zeo` notifies `StaleCache` while the entries are there, as ZEO does.
- `Cache::entries` gives each entry that a lookup can find, `(oid, start, end)`, under the writer lock, by a scan of the index and of the chain of each oid: no dropped entry, and none after `disable` or `close`. `zoe.zeo` reads it for `len()` and `contents()` of `_cache`, as ZEO's tests do.
- `Cache::clear` moves `head` two rings forward and keeps `applied`. A lost or lagged listener of a pool that does not reconnect calls `Cache::disable`: every entry goes. `Pool::disconnect` calls `Cache::close`: an anonymous mapping drops every entry, a file keeps them (below). After `disable` or `close`, the cache serves nothing (`Cache::verified` is false) and no writer touches the mapping: `store`, `apply`, `commit`, `drop_current`, `clear`, `verify` and the copy of a hit return at once. A pool that reconnects keeps the cache at the loss: the verification decides. A turn after the loss (`Turn::stale`) calls `Cache::drop_current` for each oid of its commit, not `Cache::commit`: the cache missed the commits before it.
- The cache file (ADR 0034, stage 2). `Cache::open(path, size, storage)` opens or creates the file and takes an exclusive `flock` without a wait (`File::try_lock`): a file that another open holds, in this process or another, fails with `io::ErrorKind::WouldBlock`. The file holds the header page, the index and the ring, and has that length from its creation to its close: the open gives a new file its length with `posix_fallocate`, so a write through the mapping never finds the disk full. The open reads the header page with `pread` before it maps the file, and keeps the entries only when the static words are those of the open (magic and version, size, slots, storage name) with a good static checksum, the file has the length of the layout, `head` is at least one ring, and either the clean flag is 1 with a good state checksum, or it is 0 and the boot id is the boot id of this kernel (a process died, and the page cache kept its writes). Else the open drops every entry: it makes the file zero in place (`fallocate` with `FALLOC_FL_ZERO_RANGE`, else a hole that keeps the size, else a cut to zero as a last resort) and gives it the length of the layout, so every word is zero; it makes the file shorter only when the layout is smaller, so a closed cache of this process that still maps the file does not fault. A drop never uses `head` or the index of a file that it does not trust. The open never changes a file whose first word is neither zero nor a magic `ZOECACH` of any version, such as the `.zec` file of ZEO: it fails with `io::ErrorKind::InvalidData`. An open calls `posix_fallocate` on the whole file when its blocks do not cover its length, so a kept file that a copy made sparse gets its disk space again. The mark: before the first write to the mapping, the cache writes the boot id, the clean flag 0 and a state checksum of 0, and syncs the header page (`msync`, `MS_SYNC`). The open marks a new or dropped file at once. A kept file stays clean on the disk until its first write: `set_end`, `append`, the move of `applied` and the move of `head` call `Cache::mark` under the writer lock first. So an open that keeps the file and writes nothing pays no sync, and a crash then leaves the clean file of the last close. A quick verification writes, so it pays the sync at the open; the first miss, invalidation or commit pays it otherwise. The copy of a hit, on the thread of the caller, does nothing until a write marked the file. A mark that fails disables the cache: it serves and writes nothing more, no writer tries the mark again, the file keeps the state of its last clean close, and `Pool::ready` returns at once. `Cache::fail_next_mark` (hidden) is a hook of the tests.
- A file with entries (`applied` above 0) does not serve until `Cache::verify`: `verified` is false, a lookup misses, `store` refuses, `apply` and `commit` drop the current entry of each oid and keep `applied`, so the base of the verification stays the tid of the file. A new or dropped file serves at once. `verify(tid, None)` drops every entry and sets `applied` to `tid`, also when `applied` was above it.
- The close of a marked file (`Cache::close`, and the drop of a `Cache` that was not closed) syncs the whole mapping, then writes the clean flag 1 and the state checksum, then syncs the header page, then unlocks the file with `File::unlock`: the mapping holds the open file description of the lock until the unmap, so the close of the descriptor alone does not free it. The close of a file that no write marked writes nothing, and unlocks it. A closed cache never writes its mapping again, and reads it only for `applied` and `Debug`: a later open of the same file in this process never meets a write of the closed one, and zeroes the file in place.
- The verification at the open. `Pool::start` subscribes to the listener, then reads its last tid; the dispatcher, before it serves the stream, calls `verify_file` for a cache that does not serve yet: `applied` equal to the last tid keeps the cache; below it, `Client::holds_transaction(applied)` must be true, else the server has another history and every entry goes (`FileCheck::Other`); then `getInvalidations(applied)` drops the current entry of each oid of the reply and moves `applied` to its tid, and `None` or an error drops every entry; above it, the file is ahead of the server and every entry goes, with `applied` at the last tid of the server. Nothing goes to the consumer: it starts at the last tid of the pool. A pool without a first listener runs `verify_file` in the fresh branch of `verify`. `Pool::ready` waits until the cache serves, or the close. `Pool::take_file_check` gives once how the verification ended (`FileCheck`: `Kept`, `Quick`, `Dropped`, `Failed` with the text of the error, `Other`, `Ahead`), so `zoe.zeo` logs it as ZEO does. The departures from ZEO: a file ahead of the server and a failed `getInvalidations` drop the file, where ZEO refuses the server or tries again.
- `src/cache/mapping.rs` holds the only `unsafe` code of the crate: `mmap` (anonymous and private, or of a file and shared), `msync` and `munmap` of the mapping, its view as `&[AtomicU64]`, and `posix_fallocate` of a cache file, each item with its own `#[allow(unsafe_code)]` and a `SAFETY` comment. The crate needs it for two reasons: the kernel gives the pages of an anonymous mapping as zeros only at their first write, so an unused part of the cache costs no memory; and the cache file maps through the same `Mapping`, so one code reads and writes both. Known limits: a process that cuts a cache file while a client maps it makes that client die of `SIGBUS`; the lock keeps the clients of ZOE apart, not other programs. On NFS, `flock` becomes a POSIX lock, which belongs to the process: a second open in the same process then gets the lock, and two caches write one file. `zc.lockfile` of ZEO has the same limit. The workspace lint `unsafe_code = "deny"` stays. `libc` and `xxhash-rust` are dependencies for the cache.
- tokio is allowed in this crate. `zoe-py` depends on it for the native client of `zoe.zeo.ClientStorage` (ADR 0035), so `cargo tree -p zoe-py -e normal` lists tokio. This crate never depends on rmcp.
- `zoe.zeo.ClientStorage` is a drop-in for `ZEO.ClientStorage`, never a requirement.
- A change to the wire protocol is a stop-and-ask.
